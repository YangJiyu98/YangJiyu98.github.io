# Snorkelling with a buddy: how to stay safe

**Short answer:** never snorkel alone, stay within arm's reach or easy sight of your buddy, check the conditions before you get in, and turn back while you still have energy.

## Before you get in

- **Pick a buddy and agree a plan.** Decide where you will swim, when you will turn back and what a signal for "I need help" looks like. A raised arm waved side to side is common.
- **Check conditions.** Ask a lifeguard or local operator about waves, currents, and visibility. If you are unsure, do not go.
- **Check your gear.** The mask should seal without the strap, the snorkel should be clear, and fins should fit snugly.
- **Consider a flotation vest or a tow float.** It gives you something to hold, and a bright one helps boats see you.

## In the water

- **Stay together.** Keep your buddy in sight, ideally within a few strokes. Take turns to look around for the other.
- **Watch the shore and the boats.** Boat traffic is a real risk where snorkellers float. Stay clear of boat lanes and use a bright float.
- **Mind tiredness and cold.** Currents can be stronger on the way back. Turn around before you are tired, not when you are.
- **Look, do not touch.** Do not touch or chase animals, and do not stand on coral. Some animals sting or are toxic.
- **Do not hyperventilate before breath-hold dives.** Staying on the surface is safest if you are not trained.

## Where a watch app fits

An Apple Watch can record time, distance and heart rate, and on an Ultra or a Series 10 or later, depth and water temperature. That is useful for a logbook. It is not a safety device and does not replace a human buddy.

[Finn](/finn/) is a free swim and snorkel app for Apple Watch and iPhone. A pixel animal swims with you on your wrist, and the swim goes into a logbook on your iPhone. Finn gives no warnings and no dive computer information, and it never asks you to go deeper or stay longer. Its animal pages mark the ones that sting or are toxic with a yellow "look, don't touch" triangle.
