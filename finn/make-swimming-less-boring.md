# How to make lap swimming less boring

**Short answer:** change one thing at a time. Give each swim a small job, mix your strokes, swim with someone, and add a reason to look forward to the next one.

## Ideas that work

1. **Give the swim a small job.** Today is 20 lengths of drill, tomorrow is easy and long. A clear job beats counting laps in your head.
2. **Mix it up.** Alternate strokes every four lengths, swim with a pull buoy, or try fins for one set.
3. **Break it into chunks.** Four sets of five lengths feels shorter than twenty in a row.
4. **Swim with a friend.** Even a different lane next to yours changes the feel of it. In open water, swimming with a partner is also safer.
5. **Change the water.** A new pool, a lake or the sea makes the same stroke feel new. Follow local safety advice.
6. **Track it.** Seeing a logbook fill up gives you a reason to come back. Keep the numbers kind. A streak you dread is worse than none.

## A game for your wrist

[Finn](/finn/) is a free swim and snorkel app for Apple Watch and iPhone, made for people who want time in the water to feel like play.

- A pixel animal swims with you on the watch. Everyone starts with Shelly the sea turtle.
- After ten minutes in the water, someone new might turn up, at most once an hour. There are thirty animals, and where you swim decides who.
- Your swims are saved to a logbook, with pool swims shown as one bar per length. They also go to Apple Health.
- The odds are the same for everyone, and swimming faster or farther does not change them. There are no streaks, no leaderboards and no countdowns.

For a pool, set Pool and your pool's length in the watch's Settings. Finn counts turns, so a lap pool needs walls.

Finn is for fun. It is not a training plan and not a dive computer.
