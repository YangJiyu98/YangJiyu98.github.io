# Can Apple Watch measure depth and water temperature when you snorkel?

**Short answer:** yes, on an Apple Watch with a depth sensor. That means Apple Watch Ultra, and Series 10 and later. Other watches can still time and record a swim, but they cannot read depth or water temperature.

## What each watch can do

| Watch | Depth and water temperature | Recorded: time, distance, heart rate |
|---|---|---|
| Apple Watch Ultra | Yes | Yes |
| Apple Watch Series 10 and later | Yes | Yes |
| Other Apple Watches, such as the SE 3 | No | Yes |

## Things to know

- **Snorkelling is shallow.** Most snorkelling happens in the top few metres, which suits a wrist sensor. Finn's depth reading goes to 6 metres. Below that it keeps counting time but cannot show depth.
- **Your wrist moves.** Your arm lifts out of the water now and then, so depth readings can drop to nothing between strokes. That is normal for a snorkel.
- **Water Lock.** Turn it on so the screen ignores water. Turn the Digital Crown to clear it afterwards.
- **Not for diving decisions.** A reading on your wrist is not a dive computer. Do not use it for decompression or ascent planning.

## How Finn shows it

[Finn](/finn/) is a free swim and snorkel app for Apple Watch and iPhone. On a watch with a depth sensor, its live screen shows your buddy, time in the water, depth and water temperature. The water on screen gets darker as you go down. After the swim, a snorkel in your iPhone logbook gets a depth line, and the swim's page shows the deepest point and the coldest temperature.

On a watch without a depth sensor, Finn runs in swim mode. You tap Start swim yourself, and the screen shows your buddy and the time in the water. Animals still turn up after ten minutes.

Depth is never a score in Finn. It does not change who you meet, and the app never asks you to go deeper.

## Safety

Finn is not a dive computer. It gives no decompression, no-stop or ascent information. Always swim and dive within your training, with proper instruments.
