# How to track an open-water swim on Apple Watch

**Short answer:** start an Open Water Swim workout before you get in, give the watch a clear view of the sky for a moment so GPS can find you, swim, then end the workout and check the distance. Apple Watch measures distance in open water from GPS and arm motion.

## Steps

1. **Pick the right workout.** Open water has no walls to turn at, so use an open-water swim workout, not a pool one. A pool workout counts turns and multiplies by the pool length, so it gives the wrong distance in a lake or the sea.
2. **Let GPS settle.** Stand at the water's edge with your watch in the open for a few seconds before you start. Open water swims lean on GPS and arm motion for distance.
3. **Water Lock.** Turn Water Lock on so water drops do not tap the screen. To end it, turn the Digital Crown.
4. **Swim.** Sight forward now and then and keep your arm stroke normal. The watch reads your strokes from its motion sensors.
5. **End the swim.** Turn the Crown to clear Water Lock, then end the workout. Check the distance afterwards. Open-water GPS can wander a little, so treat it as a close estimate.

## Doing it with Finn

[Finn](/finn/) is a free swim and snorkel app for Apple Watch and iPhone that records the swim for you and adds a small game on top.

- On the watch, tap **Start swim**. Finn starts an open-water swim workout and turns on Water Lock for you. On an Apple Watch Ultra or a Series 10 or later, you can also pick Finn in the watch's Settings, then General, then Auto-Launch, then When Submerged, and it opens when you go under.
- Turn the Digital Crown, then tap **End swim**. Keep going if you tapped by mistake.
- Finn saves the swim to its logbook with time, distance, pace and heart rate, and writes it to Apple Health as a swim, so it shows up in Fitness.
- Set where you swim in the watch's Settings: Open water is the default. Choose Pool only for a lap pool with walls.
- If you forget to end a swim, a watch with a depth sensor ends and saves it after fifteen minutes out of the water. Other watches keep running until you end it.
- Finn records no route. It takes one position fix at the surface to decide which animals live in the water you are in.

After ten minutes in the water, someone new might turn up and swim with your buddy. That is the game part, and it never asks you to swim faster, farther or deeper.

## Stay safe

An app is not a safety plan. Swim with a buddy or from a lifeguarded spot, wear a bright tow float in open water, and check conditions and currents first. Finn is not a dive computer or a safety device.
